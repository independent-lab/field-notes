Craft is often mistaken for polish. Polish is visible; craft is structural. It is the paragraph revised so a reader does not have to reread it, the file named so a colleague can find it, and the choice to remove a clever feature that makes the whole thing harder to use.

## The invisible audience

Every piece of work eventually reaches someone who was not in the room when it was made. Craft anticipates that person. It replaces private context with public clarity and turns good intentions into something another person can actually use.

The final ten percent is rarely glamorous. It is where naming, checking, simplifying, and explaining happen. Yet this is often the portion that determines whether work feels considerate or careless.

Craft does not require perfection. It requires attention to the handoff—the moment when something stops being ours and becomes part of someone else's day.
