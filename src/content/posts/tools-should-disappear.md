A tool begins as a promise: this will make the difficult thing easier. Over time, many tools quietly reverse the arrangement. They ask to be configured, updated, organized, and checked. The work becomes secondary to managing the system meant to support it.

## Measure the return of attention

Speed is an incomplete measure of usefulness. A tool can make one action faster while scattering the rest of the day. A better question is whether it returns more attention than it consumes.

The best tools recede during use. Their logic becomes legible, their boundaries become familiar, and the person can stay with the task instead of negotiating with the interface.

This does not mean technology should be invisible. It means its presence should be proportional to the job. A pencil can be beautiful, but it does not interrupt the sentence to announce itself.
