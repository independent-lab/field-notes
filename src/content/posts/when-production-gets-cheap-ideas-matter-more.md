My original goal was not to become a writer. It was to provide ideas, opinions, observations and source material, then let an AI-assisted publishing system handle much of what stood between those inputs and a finished publication.

The system could assist with research, produce a draft, improve structure, format the article, generate metadata, update the site and prepare a deployment. I would supply the underlying thought and approve the result.

This arrangement is appealing partly because production contains many tasks that are necessary but not, for me, the main point. A strong idea can disappear inside the effort required to turn it into a finished object. Remove enough of that effort and more ideas have a chance to become visible.

But lower production costs create a larger question: if making something becomes dramatically easier, what becomes scarce?

Not pages. Not posts. Not competent sentences or polished layouts. Those become abundant quickly.

The scarce elements are judgment, perspective, curiosity, taste and an idea that originated in a particular mind for a particular reason.

## Production and authorship are different jobs

Publishing has always combined at least two kinds of work. There is the work of deciding what is worth saying, and there is the work of turning that decision into an artifact other people can read.

The first includes noticing, questioning, choosing a position and accepting responsibility for it. The second includes researching, drafting, editing, formatting, producing and distributing. In practice the two overlap. Editing can change an argument; research can overturn an opinion; the constraints of a form can reveal whether an idea is as substantial as it first appeared.

Still, the distinction is useful because AI changes the cost of the second category much faster than it changes the value of the first.

An AI system can take a rough thesis and propose a structure. It can identify missing context, improve transitions and create versions for different formats. A coding agent can place the finished article into a content collection, connect an image, run a static-site build and update a deployment. These are meaningful capabilities. They compress a chain of specialized tasks into a guided conversation and a review process.

What the system should not do is invent the human premise. It should not fabricate the experience that supposedly produced an opinion. It should not quietly make the argument more fashionable, more certain or more dramatic than the person behind it believes.

The human contribution is not merely the prompt. It is the decision to pursue this idea rather than another, the judgment applied to evidence, the taste that rejects an adequate but generic phrase and the approval that says: yes, this represents what I mean.

The machine can help make the object. Authorship remains the responsibility for what the object says.

## Abundance changes the bottleneck

When an expensive part of a process becomes cheap, attention moves to whatever still limits the outcome. If a publication no longer requires direct command of web development, layout software or a complicated content-management system, more people can publish. That is an opportunity.

People with deep experience but little interest in production can make their knowledge accessible. A person can test an idea in public without first building an organization around it. Small, specific publications can exist because their economics no longer require a large audience. The distance between observation and artifact becomes shorter.

Yet abundance does not distinguish between ideas that needed a lower barrier and ideas that did not need to exist.

The same tools can produce endless articles assembled from familiar claims, organized into familiar sections and concluded with familiar confidence. Each piece may be readable. Together they create the sensation of walking through an airport where every gate leads to the same lounge.

Generic AI content is not dangerous because it is always false or badly written. Its more ordinary danger is that it consumes attention without adding perspective. It looks like communication while functioning as surface area.

As production becomes cheaper, publishing something is weaker evidence that someone had something to say.

## The value of a point of view

Originality does not require a thought that has never occurred in human history. That would be an unreasonable standard and a reliable way to publish nothing.

A point of view can be original because of the combination: this person's experience, applied to this situation, producing this judgment. It can come from noticing a contradiction others have accepted, connecting ideas from different domains or stating clearly what a group has sensed but not articulated.

This is why curiosity matters. Curiosity determines which details become inputs. Judgment decides what those details mean. Taste shapes how much to include, what to leave unresolved and when a polished result still feels hollow.

AI can support each of these faculties, but support is not substitution. It can offer questions, but the human decides which question is alive. It can find sources, but the human judges whether they are credible and relevant. It can generate alternatives, but the human recognizes the one that sounds true rather than merely finished.

That last distinction may become increasingly important. Fluency used to be a useful signal of effort and sometimes expertise. When fluency is readily generated, readers will need other signals: specificity, evidence, intellectual honesty, unusual observation and a consistent relationship between what a person says and what they appear to value.

## A division of labor worth protecting

The publishing system I want has a clear division of responsibility.

The human provides the idea, judgment, opinion, taste and final approval. The machine assists with research, drafting, formatting, production, deployment and iteration.

This is not a permanent technical boundary. AI systems will become more capable, and the practical division will change. It is a boundary of authorship. The goal is to automate the mechanics while retaining a person who is answerable for the underlying thinking.

That also means resisting automation when it weakens the work. Sometimes writing the draft is how the person discovers the idea. Sometimes the awkward paragraph contains a distinction that a smoother version would erase. Sometimes efficiency is the wrong objective because the friction is doing intellectual work.

The system should make production available, not make deliberation optional.

If it succeeds, more people will be able to turn useful thought into durable form. If it fails, we will have an extraordinary apparatus for producing material that no one particularly meant.

Cheap production does not guarantee better ideas. It raises the return on having one.
