Mass media rewards the sentence that can travel furthest. Small publications can choose a different ambition: to say something precise enough that the right reader feels directly addressed.

## Correspondence at a distance

An independent publication is a peculiar kind of letter. Its sender may not know who will open it, but the voice can still be intimate. The writing does not need to flatten itself into universality; it can keep its local detail, its unusual preoccupations, and its unfinished edges.

Smallness is not automatically virtuous. It is simply a useful constraint. Without the demand to please everyone, a publication can develop taste—and taste is one of the reasons readers return.

The goal is not obscurity. It is recognition: fewer people, perhaps, but a clearer exchange between the writer and the person on the other side of the page.
