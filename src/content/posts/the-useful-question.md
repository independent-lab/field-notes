Some questions preserve the shape of a problem. “How can I do all of this?” assumes that all of it must be done. “How can we make this faster?” assumes speed is the missing quality. The answer may improve the situation while leaving its weakest premise untouched.

## Questions are frames

A useful question changes what can be seen. What can be removed? Who is this actually for? What would happen if we waited? These questions do not guarantee wisdom, but they allow alternatives into the room.

The habit is simple: when an answer feels cramped, inspect the question around it. The frame may be doing more work than the facts.
