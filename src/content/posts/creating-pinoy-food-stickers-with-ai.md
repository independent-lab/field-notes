> **From the archive**  
> Originally published on Siklab Serye · October 21, 2023

Filipino cuisine is full of distinctive shapes, textures, and colors. Pinoy spaghetti, adobo, balut, kwek-kwek, isaw, sinigang, and lechon became the starting point for an experiment: could DALL·E turn written descriptions of familiar food into a coherent set of sticker ideas?

I began by describing the visible components of each dish, aiming to capture not only its appearance but some of its character. The prompts produced a manga-inspired series:

## The dishes

**Pinoy spaghetti.** Sweet and savory sauce, hotdog slices, ground meat, and grated cheese became a bright plate of curling noodles.

**Adobo.** Pieces of meat in a soy sauce and vinegar marinade appeared with garlic and bay leaves.

**Balut.** The illustration focused on the opened shell and the delicacy inside.

**Kwek-kwek.** Golden battered quail eggs were arranged on sticks with a vinegar dip.

**Isaw.** The skewers took on a smoky, lightly charred appearance.

**Sinigang.** A steaming pot gathered vegetables and meat in a tamarind-based broth.

**Lechon.** A whole roasted pig became the center of the series, rendered with glossy, crisp-looking skin.

The exercise showed how much a prompt depends on observation. Naming the dish was only the beginning; the useful details were the ingredients, textures, presentation, and small visual signals that made one image read differently from another.

At the time, seeing those descriptions return as a related set of images felt like an invitation to experiment. The results were not a replacement for a human illustrator or for the food itself. They were sketches for what the stickers might become—and a record of an early moment when image generation suddenly felt accessible.
